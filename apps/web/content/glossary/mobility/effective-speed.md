The speed a vehicle can actually hold on a given ground type, rather than the cap printed on its specification sheet.

Top speed is a limit the engine may never reach. Effective speed is what the engine, the weight and the ground resistance produce together, computed per terrain type, and it is usually well below the quoted figure on anything soft.

It is the number that explains why a vehicle rated at 55 kilometres per hour crawls across a field while another rated at 45 crosses it comfortably.
