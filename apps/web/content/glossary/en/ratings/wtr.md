Wargaming's current rating, computed per vehicle from what a player does in battle and deliberately blind to whether the battle was won.

WTR scores each vehicle separately, then rolls those scores into one account figure. Depending on the vehicle's role it reads up to four things: damage dealt and assisted, damage blocked, vehicles destroyed, and survival. A heavy is judged partly on what it absorbs, a scout on what it makes possible, so the same account can score very differently on two vehicles it plays equally well.

Each vehicle's score has two halves. A quarter of it is where the player stands among everyone on the server who plays that same vehicle, criterion by criterion, each weighted for the vehicle. The other three quarters are how far above that vehicle's server average they perform, survival left out. Either way the yardstick is the vehicle's own population, never a fixed target, which is why a strong game on a vehicle few people play well moves the score more than the same game on a popular one.

The account figure is an average of the vehicle scores, weighted by the battles played on each, by tier and by role. On top of that, an account that holds a very high score (about 5,300 and up) on at least one vehicle, over enough battles, gets a bonus on its whole rating.

Two practical consequences follow. A vehicle with a thousand battles on it barely moves any more: a hundred excellent games on top of a weak record lift that vehicle by a few hundred points at most. And the vehicles still under a few hundred battles are where a weak score is cheapest to repair.

Wargaming publishes the structure of the formula but not its constants: which criteria each vehicle reads, their weights, and the scaling. Combined with the fact that every score is measured against the whole server, that means WTR cannot be recomputed from outside the game, per vehicle or over a recent window. It can only be read as Wargaming publishes it.

Victories and defeats are not part of it. That is the deliberate difference with Personal Rating, which counts battles and wins and therefore keeps climbing as long as an account keeps playing.

The game shows it as a band (Bronze through Legend) rather than as a number, and only once an account has at least 50 random battles. It is replacing Personal Rating as the rating the client displays.
