How quickly a vehicle reaches speed from a standstill, which matters far more in practice than its top speed.

Most driving in a battle is short: out of cover, across a gap, back behind a rock. All of it is acceleration, and a vehicle that reaches 40 kilometres per hour in three seconds beats one that reaches 55 in twelve.

It comes from power-to-weight divided by terrain resistance, and it is the reason the first position of a battle is contested by mediums and lights rather than by whichever vehicle has the highest number on its speedometer.
